# Mastery units: draft boundary review

Generated by `go run ./cmd/masteryunits`. **This is a draft partition of the existing concept DAG, not a**
**second hierarchy, and not an implementation of assessments.** Unit prerequisite order
below is *derived from the concept DAG*; it is not stored in the JSON.

Rule: `unit = (domain, floor(conceptDepth / bandWidth)); conceptDepth = the concept's level in the concept DAG's own topological order`
Band width: **3** — a draft parameter. The open question is how often a learner should be
assessed; `3` is a candidate, not a decision. Regenerate with `-band 2` or `-band 4`.

## Band width sweep

The width is the open decision, so here is every candidate rather than one number. The
columns that matter are **singletons** (§12 forbids a quiz per concept) and **max**, since a
128-concept unit is not assessable either. Depth is per-concept here, so backward edges are 0
at every width.

| 15 | 40 | 28 | 115 | 2 | 3 | 20 | 0 |
| 20 | 33 | 35 | 205 | 1 | 2 | 21 | 0 |
| 45 | 21 | 46 | 205 | 0 | 0 | 21 | 0 |

**No width is clean.** Small widths fragment — at 3 there are 21 singleton units, which is a
quiz per concept and is exactly what §12 rules out. Large widths stop fragmenting but produce
units too large to assess: at 45 the partition is just the 17 domains themselves, whose
largest is 128 concepts. The usable widths trade fragmentation against size rather than
resolving it, so
**this rule alone does not produce assessment-grade boundaries** and the partition needs hand
merging on top of a derived draft. That is the finding D3 was asked to produce.

## Why depth is measured per concept

Banding on *subdomain* depth was the first candidate and it does not work on this corpus.
The subdomain condensation of the concept DAG **contains cycles**: grouping concepts by
subdomain discards the per-concept ordering, so a concept in subdomain A can be a
prerequisite of one in subdomain B whose other members depend on A.

**165 of 125 subdomains cannot be levelled** — they sit inside a cycle, or downstream
of one, so no depth function exists for them at all. (Tarjan puts 26 inside cycles and
the remaining 34 behind them; Kahn cannot order either group, because ordering a node
requires its predecessors to be ordered first.) An implementation that ignores this
gets level 0 for all of them, which is a confidently wrong answer rather than a missing
one — which is what the first Go version of this generator did.

## Not decided here

- The band width, which is the assessment frequency.
- The pass threshold (D4): a fixed proportion correct, versus requiring the assessment's
  answers to clear `mastery.MasteryThreshold`. This report deliberately implies neither.
- Whether a unit of one concept is an error or a content judgement.
- Everything downstream: assessment storage, migrations, pass/fail, the progression gate,
  retake policy, and any dashboard change.
